## 17 · Observations

Facts worth a decision, in the order they would cost something.

1. **`100206` is below its minimum and is the single constraint on this
   formula.** 0.7768 kg against a minimum of 3.000. It caps production at 28.8
   litres and it caps every other formula that uses prepared licorice. It is
   made in house and the materials for 6.5 kg are on the shelf.

2. **Stock of `209016` is above its maximum** — 9.5998 L against 8.000, after a
   run that yielded 12.25 L where 10 were planned. If the maximum is right, the
   run was too big; if the run was right, the maximum is stale.

3. **`100206`'s procurement method says Buy, and it is made.** `PrcrmntMtd = B`
   with `TreeType = P`, and every receipt in its history is a production
   receipt. The ₪1,000/kg in *last purchase price* was stamped the same day as
   a production receipt and is not a price anybody paid a supplier.

4. **The BOM's own prices are three years behind the item cards.** Any costing
   read off the bill of materials understates the formula by about ₪34 a litre.

5. **Yields beat the plan in every run that produced anything.** Twelve runs,
   none under plan, five over it. Either the recipe under-states its yield or
   the plan quantities are entered conservatively — worth asking the lab which.

6. **The card says 30% alcohol; the recipe takes 0.42 L of 96% per litre.**
   Those two do not restate each other, and the gap is presumably cooking and
   evaporation. Stated here as two facts, not as a contradiction.

7. **`990009` בישול - לא פעיל** is a process line carried as a backflushed item
   with no stock and a name that says "inactive" while its card says active. It
   works, but a new system modelling labour steps will have to decide whether to
   keep carrying them as items.
